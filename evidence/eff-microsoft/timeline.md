# EFF - Microsoft Evidence Timeline

This timeline preserves dated observations separately from pattern interpretation.

## 1993
EFF archive material documents Rob Glaser, then a Microsoft consultant and former Microsoft vice president, joining the EFF board. Historical provenance only.

## March 2014
EFF publishes direct criticism of Microsoft's contractual authority to access user content following Microsoft's search of a Hotmail account during a leak investigation.

EFF states that it had communicated directly with Microsoft and commends Microsoft's policy change.

Microsoft proposes broader reform work involving Microsoft, EFF, CDT, and other Internet companies.

## May 2014
EFF awards Microsoft six of six stars in its government-data-access/privacy scorecard.

Microsoft publicly promotes the favorable EFF rating.

## 2014-2018
EFF and Microsoft repeatedly align on litigation and advocacy concerning government surveillance, secrecy restrictions, warrants, and cross-border access to user data.

## 2015
EFF's scorecard reduces Microsoft's rating, demonstrating continued willingness to criticize Microsoft in government-access/privacy dimensions.

## 2017
EFF publicly discusses Microsoft litigation as an example of a technology company standing up for users against government secrecy restrictions.

## 2018
EFF legal briefing discusses Microsoft Terms alongside the broader problem of private companies unilaterally defining contractual privacy expectations.

## October 2020
Microsoft Services Agreement includes authority reserving Microsoft's ability to review user content in connection with investigating agreement violations.

## 2021
EFF/ACLU litigation briefing explicitly cites the 2020 Microsoft Services Agreement and the content-review provision.

## 2023-2026
Microsoft continues issuing Services Agreement revisions. In the present case review, no comparable Microsoft-specific EFF consumer-facing Terms-of-Service intervention has yet been located.

## Present operational dependency
EFF publicly documents use of Microsoft Power Pages, Power Platform, and Dynamics for member/contact infrastructure.

## Present mediated giving path
Microsoft Rewards exposes EFF as an eligible charitable destination through a mediated rewards/donation mechanism.
