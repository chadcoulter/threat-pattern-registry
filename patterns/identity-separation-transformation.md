# Identity Separation Transformation

## Why It Is Important to Understand Identity Separation

Identity theft is often treated as a problem of stolen credentials, forged documents, compromised accounts, or fraudulent transactions. Those are visible manifestations of a deeper systemic problem: **the separation of an identity from the provenance that establishes who or what actually produced an action, statement, record, or relationship.**

Understanding that separation matters because a victim is not merely trying to prove that someone else used their name. They are trying to repair a corrupted identity graph.

```text
authentic person
-> authentic history
-> authentic control
-> authentic actions
```

has been contaminated by:

```text
unauthorized actor
-> acquired identifier
-> false association
-> action attributed to victim
```

Once a system begins treating the false association as part of the victim's identity, the problem can propagate through credit records, databases, automated decisions, institutional records, reputation systems, and other dependent systems.

Countering identity theft therefore requires more than denying the fraudulent action. It requires reconstructing provenance:

```text
establish source
-> restore continuity
-> distinguish actors
-> sever false associations
-> reconnect records to their actual source
```

Understanding the underlying transformation gives victims, investigators, institutions, and automated systems a way to reason about **how identity was misappropriated and how that association can be reversed.**

## Abstract

**Identity Separation Transformation** describes a systemic process in which information, attributes, actions, authority, or identifiers are detached from the provenance that establishes their source.

The basic transformation is:

```text
source
+ identity
+ context
+ provenance
+ expression

        |
        v

identity separation

        |
        v

expression / attribute
without reliable source attachment

        |
        v

portability

        |
        v

rebinding to another identity

        |
        v

normalization of the new association
```

Once an attribute has been separated from its source, it becomes easier to attach that attribute to another identity.

The process can therefore become a vehicle for **identity misappropriation**:

```text
IDENTITY
-> SEPARATION
-> PORTABILITY
-> REBINDING
-> NORMALIZATION
-> MISAPPROPRIATION
```

The appropriated element may include a name, credential, action, statement, authorship, authority, reputation, history, symbolic role, relationship, or other identity-bearing property.

The defining condition is not simply that information moved.

It is that the provenance relationship changed:

```text
original:

A -> X
```

becomes:

```text
separation:

A -X-> X
```

and then:

```text
rebinding:

B -> X
```

until the system eventually resolves:

```text
B = source of X
```

The information may remain substantially unchanged while the **identity associated with that information has been transformed**.

Identity Separation Transformation therefore provides a general model for examining identity misappropriation across personal identity theft, institutional records, authorship, historical attribution, digital systems, AI-generated information, and other systems in which identity depends upon durable provenance.

## Transformation Process

The broader systemic transformation can be represented as:

```text
SOURCE STATE

source
+ identity
+ context
+ provenance
+ expression

        |
        v

1. IDENTITY-SOURCE SEPARATION

detach the expression, action, attribute,
authority, or identifier from its source identity

        |
        v

2. PROVENANCE WEAKENING OR REMOVAL

remove, obscure, overwrite, or make inaccessible
the evidence that establishes the original relationship

        |
        v

3. PORTABILITY

the detached element can now move independently
of the source that originally gave it identity

        |
        v

4. REBINDING

attach the detached element to another identity

        |
        v

5. NORMALIZATION

repeat, record, or propagate the new association
until downstream systems begin treating it as ordinary

        |
        v

6. IDENTITY MISAPPROPRIATION

the attributes, actions, expressions, authority,
or history of one source are persistently
attributed to another
```

## Identity Misappropriation

**Identity misappropriation occurs when identity-source separation allows the attributes, expressions, actions, authority, or symbolic properties of one source to become persistently attributed to another.**

The appropriated object does not need to be a legal identity or a person's literal name. It may be:

- authorship
- voice
- authority
- reputation
- credentials
- history
- actions
- statements
- symbols
- teachings
- relationships
- lineage
- institutional role
- recorded ownership or control

Ordinary quotation, adaptation, inheritance, and attribution preserve the provenance edge:

```text
B receives X from A
```

Identity misappropriation weakens or removes that edge:

```text
A -> X

A -X-> X

B -> X
```

Eventually the transformed system may resolve:

```text
B = source of X
```

The information has not necessarily been destroyed.

**Its identity relationship has been transformed.**

## Defensive Inverse Transform

Countering identity misappropriation requires reversing the transformation.

```text
MISAPPROPRIATED STATE

false identity association
+ detached provenance
+ propagated records

        |
        v

1. ESTABLISH SOURCE

identify the actual person, system,
or actor from which the relevant identity originates

        |
        v

2. RECONSTRUCT PROVENANCE

recover records, timestamps, control history,
relationships, transactions, witnesses,
and other continuity evidence

        |
        v

3. DISTINGUISH ACTORS

separate the authentic source from
the unauthorized or falsely associated actor

        |
        v

4. SEVER FALSE BINDINGS

identify records and systems that incorrectly
associate the victim with actions of another actor

        |
        v

5. REBIND ACTIONS TO THEIR ACTUAL SOURCE

restore the correct provenance relationship

        |
        v

6. RESTORE IDENTITY INTEGRITY

propagate the corrected association through
the systems that inherited the false one
```

The practical governing principle is:

> **Identity theft succeeds when systems lose the ability to distinguish an identity from someone who has acquired its identifiers. Countering it requires restoring the provenance that connects the identity to its actual source.**

## Compact Transform

```text
Identity
-> Separation
-> Provenance Loss
-> Portability
-> Rebinding
-> Normalization
-> Misappropriation
```

Inverse:

```text
Misappropriation
-> Provenance Recovery
-> Actor Separation
-> False-Binding Removal
-> Correct Rebinding
-> Identity Restoration
```
