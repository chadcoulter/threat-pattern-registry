# Recovered Texts

## Purpose

This working document collects texts and textual fragments recovered for preservation, comparison, reconstruction, and later analysis.

This material is separate from the threat-pattern work in this repository. Its purpose is textual recovery: preserving information that may be incomplete, difficult to locate, fragmented across sources, or otherwise at risk of being lost from the working record.

## Working Corpus

### Sefer HaYashar / Book of Jasher

Source file: `Sefer_HaYashar_Book_of_Jasher.txt`

The recovered source is organized by chapter. The surviving extraction is uneven: some chapters contain the underlying text while others contain source-retrieval error material rather than the chapter text itself. This makes it useful both as a recovered text and as a reconstruction target.

### Book of Gad the Seer

Source file: `Book_of_Gad_the_Seer.txt`

The recovered source contains the text titled *Ancient Book of Gad the Seer* and identifies the included translation text as public domain while distinguishing the associated commentary and charts as copyrighted material.

Its contents are organized into fourteen sections, including the First Vision, Second Vision, David-related narratives, Psalms 145 and 144, David's Death, Tamar, and the Great Tribulation.

## Recovery Model

Recovered texts should be preserved in layers rather than silently normalized.

```text
source artifact
-> recovered text
-> missing / damaged / inaccessible regions identified
-> alternate witnesses or fragments added
-> reconstruction candidates recorded
-> provenance retained
```

A recovered text may therefore contain:

- intact passages;
- partial passages;
- retrieval failures;
- formatting residue;
- competing readings;
- later reconstruction candidates;
- source and provenance notes.

The recovery process should preserve the distinction between what was present in the source artifact and what is added during reconstruction.

## Working Principle

Do not discard information merely because the surviving text is incomplete.

Preserve what survives, mark the gaps, and allow later sources to restore structure around them.
