# Institutional Dependency Evidence Model

This model defines the evidentiary states used to distinguish ordinary advocacy reprioritization from an Institutional Dependency Blind Spot and, at higher thresholds, dependency-induced distortion or capture.

## States

```text
REPRIORITIZATION
      |
      v
ANOMALOUS
      |
      v
POTENTIAL_BLIND_SPOT
      |
      v
CORROBORATED_BLIND_SPOT
      |
      v
STRONGLY_CORROBORATED
      |
      v
DEPENDENCY_INDUCED_DISTORTION
      |
      v
CAPTURED
```

## Transition Tests

### REPRIORITIZATION -> ANOMALOUS

Requires selective reduction in scrutiny relative to comparable actors or comparable conduct.

### ANOMALOUS -> POTENTIAL_BLIND_SPOT

Requires a meaningful dependency or strategic alignment plus temporal correlation between relationship formation and selective reduction in scrutiny.

### POTENTIAL_BLIND_SPOT -> CORROBORATED_BLIND_SPOT

Requires either:

- counterfactual asymmetry: materially comparable conduct receives stronger scrutiny when performed by less-dependent peers, or
- knowledge without comparable action: evidence shows the organization knew of the conduct and possessed the framework/capacity to address it.

### CORROBORATED_BLIND_SPOT -> STRONGLY_CORROBORATED

Requires evidence that the missing scrutiny maps specifically to the relationship boundary while criticism continues in adjacent or aligned domains.

### STRONGLY_CORROBORATED -> DEPENDENCY_INDUCED_DISTORTION

Requires causal evidence that dependency affected a decision, publication, campaign, investigation, or other advocacy behavior.

### DEPENDENCY_INDUCED_DISTORTION -> CAPTURED

Requires explicit evidence of control, condition, exchange, suppression, contractual influence, quid pro quo, or equivalent direction.

## Conservative Rule

A case must remain at the lowest state supported by evidence. Higher states may not be inferred from lower-state indicators alone.

Silence is not capture. Dependency is not capture. Correlation is not causal influence. Each transition requires its own evidence.
