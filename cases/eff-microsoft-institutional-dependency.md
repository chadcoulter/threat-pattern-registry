# EFF - Microsoft Institutional Dependency Case

## Current Classification

`POTENTIAL_BLIND_SPOT`

The present evidence supports a structural dependency-and-scrutiny anomaly, but the case should not be elevated to corroborated until a peer/control comparison is completed or equivalent counterfactual evidence is established.

## Core Observation

EFF published direct consumer-facing criticism of Microsoft's contractual authority over user content in March 2014. Microsoft changed policy and proposed broader reform work involving EFF, CDT, and other companies. In subsequent years, EFF and Microsoft repeatedly aligned in surveillance and government-access litigation and policy work.

EFF continued to discuss and evaluate Microsoft, but the public emphasis shifted heavily toward Microsoft as an ally against government access rather than Microsoft as a contractual counterparty exercising power over consumers.

EFF later cited Microsoft Terms/Services Agreement provisions in litigation, demonstrating continued awareness of the underlying contractual authority, while a comparable Microsoft-specific consumer-facing Terms-of-Service intervention has not yet been located for the later agreement changes reviewed in this case.

## Timeline

### 2014-03
EFF publishes "Reforming Terms of Service: Microsoft Changes Its Policy on Access to User Data," criticizing broad contractual powers to access user content and noting direct communication with Microsoft.

### 2014
Microsoft changes policy and proposes an industry reform process involving EFF and CDT.

### 2014
EFF gives Microsoft 6/6 in its privacy/government-data-access scorecard; Microsoft publicly promotes the rating.

### 2014-2018
EFF and Microsoft repeatedly align in litigation and advocacy around government surveillance, secrecy, and cross-border data access.

### 2018
EFF legal briefing discusses Microsoft Terms and the broader problem of constitutional rights depending on private providers' unilateral form contracts.

### 2020
Microsoft Services Agreement contains contractual content-review authority.

### 2021
EFF/ACLU briefing explicitly cites Microsoft's 2020 Services Agreement and quotes the content-review authority in litigation.

### 2023-2026
Microsoft continues revising its Services Agreement, including AI/content/moderation-related provisions. No comparable Microsoft-specific EFF consumer Terms campaign has yet been located in this case review.

### Present
EFF publicly documents use of Microsoft Power Pages, Power Platform, and Dynamics for member/contact operations, creating a current infrastructure/platform dependency vector.

## Identified Dependency Vectors

### LEGAL
Recurring alignment in surveillance, secrecy, warrant, and cross-border data-access litigation.

### POLICY
2014 reform collaboration following EFF criticism of Microsoft's access-to-user-data policy.

### REPUTATIONAL
EFF privacy ratings praised Microsoft; Microsoft in turn publicly promoted favorable EFF ratings.

### INFRASTRUCTURE / PLATFORM
EFF currently documents Microsoft Power Pages, Power Platform, and Dynamics in its member/contact infrastructure.

### FINANCIAL - MEDIATED
Microsoft Rewards provides a pathway for users to direct reward value toward EFF through a mediated charitable mechanism. This is not presently classified as direct Microsoft institutional funding.

### PERSONNEL - HISTORICAL
EFF archives document historical Microsoft personnel crossover through former Microsoft executive/consultant Rob Glaser joining the EFF board in the early 1990s. This is retained as historical provenance, not evidence of present influence.

## Evidence Supporting Potential Blind Spot

- meaningful public scrutiny of Microsoft contractual authority before/at relationship pivot
- relationship/policy collaboration forms immediately after criticism
- repeated subsequent legal and policy alignment
- EFF continues to know and cite Microsoft contractual provisions
- Microsoft-specific consumer ToS advocacy appears materially thinner afterward
- present Microsoft infrastructure dependency exists

## Evidence Still Required for Corroborated State

- systematic comparison against EFF treatment of materially comparable Google, Apple, Meta, Amazon, or other platform terms
- quantified scrutiny intensity before and after relationship formation
- evidence showing equivalent conduct by peers received materially stronger EFF response
- or stronger evidence that EFF deliberately declined Microsoft-specific scrutiny despite documented awareness

## Prohibited Inference at Current State

This record does not establish bribery, corruption, conscious suppression, quid pro quo, or actual institutional capture.
